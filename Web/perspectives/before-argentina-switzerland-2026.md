# Before Argentina vs. Switzerland

*Houyi Liu*

![Lionel Messi playing for Argentina at the 2022 FIFA World Cup](perspectives/images/messi-argentina-2022-world-cup.jpg)

> I never deliberately think about whether tonight might be your last World Cup match, but I know that day will eventually come.

## Eight Years, One Journey

I have never deliberately asked myself whether tonight will be your last game at a World Cup. But I know that day will come. The harder question is how I should write the story of these eight years, the story of my own life beside your World Cup journey.

I did not see your lonely back at the Maracana in 2014 after the missed one-on-one and the final chance for an equalizer. I did not see the look in your eyes when the World Cup trophy was so close and yet so impossibly far away. The first World Cup I watched with you in it was Russia 2018.

I can still vaguely remember the missed penalty against Iceland, the draw that followed, and the wave of ridicule after Croatia beat Argentina 3-0. I also remember the force that erupted when you received Rojo's pass, controlled the ball, shifted it once, and fired. But in that tournament, the team standing in your way was France, the eventual champion.

Otamendi's mistake gave France its first penalty. Pavard's impossible long-range strike felt like He Wei's commentary brought to life, a sword falling through peach-blossom shadows and cutting off Argentina's hope of advancing. Near the end, I remember you twisting and turning inside the box, beating every defender in front of you, only to produce a soft, powerless shot at Lloris.

After the match, the camera found you still standing on the pitch. Your hands were on your waist. You looked into the distance while the sunset spread over the open roof of Kazan Arena. In that moment, I truly felt that you had grown old, and that the first World Cup I watched you play might also be the last.

I cried then. That summer was not only the World Cup. It was also my first trip to the United States. On the flight from Guangzhou to San Francisco, my headphones kept playing "Don't Cry for Me Argentina."

## The Prayer Before Another Beginning

Time moved on. The next time I saw you in a major national-team tournament was the 2021 Copa America. Again, it was the Maracana. Again, the opponent was Brazil.

The pressure of Argentina's long wait for a Copa America title made even me, watching through a screen, feel uneasy. Then, in the 33rd minute, Angel Di Maria delivered a chip so beautiful that it felt perfect. The ball opened Ederson's goal. At that moment, I still did not know that this former Real Madrid winger, someone I had once disliked, would make me cry in Qatar one year later.

After the match, the whole team could hardly hold back its tears. Because of the pandemic, the vast stadium had only a small number of spectators. You sat in the middle of the pitch, your eyes red, speaking with your family on the phone. I stayed in front of my screen too, connected with a friend on voice chat, unwilling to turn it off even after CCTV cut away from the live feed.

It was the morning before my first mock exam before the gaokao. Before handing in my phone, I posted to WeChat Moments: "It is time to pray for another Leo."

By then, my affection for you seemed to have gone beyond football skill. It was more like a resonance with life. I believe that being a good person, a pure and upright person, will eventually be worthy of fate's kindness. Perhaps, in some unseen way, the road to the 2022 title had already begun there.

## A Winter in Qatar

2022 was the year of my gaokao. In June, during the Finalissima, you carried Di Lorenzo and sent out an assist. I had already begun looking forward to the winter feast of football by the Persian Gulf.

That winter was unforgettable. I was newly in college and confused. Pandemic control was oppressive, and the virus brought physical suffering. In the first group-stage match, the school organized a watch party in the gym. A large crowd of Argentina fans gathered there. The screen was blurry, and the November wind near the exit carried a thin chill, but none of that reduced people's enthusiasm.

When the first goal came, the whole place instantly caught fire. Everyone felt that this goal had opened your final World Cup journey in the right way. But the reversal came too quickly. Defensive lapses, a closed-off attack, and hidden problems gradually surfaced. The 2-1 loss to Saudi Arabia cast a dark shadow over the team's future.

After the match, public opinion came crashing down. Even though you asked the fans to believe, the humiliating result made it hard to imagine that Argentina could go far. There was also a Ronaldo fan at the scene. Every time Saudi Arabia built an attack, he clapped and cheered. At that age I still took these things very seriously. After the final whistle, when the whole place fell silent, his applause sounded especially harsh. I even wanted to go up and confront him.

But I knew that in competitive sports, when you lose, you are the weaker side. We had no position from which to argue. And I did not really believe that this Argentina team looked like a champion. It seemed, once again, that Messi's last World Cup might become another tragic series.

Unable to bear the pain of the loss, I walked loop after loop around the SWUFE campus while talking to a friend on the phone. My father also called to comfort me. He told me not to care too much about outside voices. Loving Messi, he said, does not mean only loving him when he wins.

## The Shot Against Mexico

The next match was against Mexico. Because of the first-round defeat, Argentina needed three points to keep the possibility of advancing. It is no exaggeration to call it a life-or-death match.

After 60 hard minutes, neither side had scored. The pressure grew heavier and heavier, and the whole team had clearly fallen into a restless trap. Then Angel moved across from the right and found you with precision in a little pocket of space. You shot immediately. The ball skimmed along the grass, passed Ochoa's dive, and rolled into the net.

From that moment, the team seemed to have survived disaster. An enormous potential was released. Enzo stepped his bicycle move toward the box and ended the suspense with an almost perfect curling shot.

I no longer remember whether that match happened while I was in isolation. I only remember that after the win, I could not hide my excitement. I posted to WeChat Moments: "Maybe I no longer care how far you can go, but tonight I am truly happy."

Those two simple words, truly happy, are something only Messi fans who have watched the whole road can fully understand.

## The Night I Started to Believe

Argentina finished first in the group after danger and doubt, beat Australia with everything it had, and then met an old rival: the Netherlands. Eight years earlier, Mascherano's desperate tackle, the one that tore him open, was still vivid in memory. This was always going to be a confrontation of history and fate.

Honestly, no one knew before the match which side was stronger. By then, pandemic control was nearing its end. The school had lifted isolation and departure restrictions. All of my roommates had already left. The match kicked off at 3 a.m., and my College English Test Band 4 exam was at 8 a.m.

In a strange state of mind, I opened the livestream alone in an extremely quiet dorm room. Before halftime, on a flank the broadcast director almost missed, you delivered an unsolvable pass. The ball first went through Ake's legs, then slipped through the narrow limit of De Jong's reach, arriving perfectly at Molina's feet. In that tiny margin, Van Dijk had already thrown his whole body out and still could not break it up.

1-0.

That assist, later called a "God's-eye-view" pass, left the commentators speechless. Even when the aerial replay came, they still seemed to have no words for its beauty. Watching live, all I could do was hammer my bed.

The match, of course, did not go smoothly. In the final moment of extra time, Enzo's long-range shot hit the post, and the game entered the cruelest stage: penalties.

In 2014, Argentina had also faced the Netherlands, and Romero had saved two Dutch penalties. Now the goalkeeper was Dibu Martinez. I had seen what he did against Colombia in the Copa America, so I did not doubt his penalty-saving ability. But in that scene, it was impossible not to be nervous.

The late-November cold wind blew into the dorm, making a room that already had only me in it feel even colder. With adrenaline carrying me, goosebumps rose across my body. My hands trembled under a thin blanket. When Lautaro converted the last penalty, that thrilling night finally ended.

It was also on that night that I began to believe: we really could be champions.

## Fever Before the Final

After Argentina crossed Croatia, the semester was ending. Universities across the country had reopened. Old friends returned to Chengdu from different places, but the shadow of the pandemic had not fully gone away.

On the Friday before the final, I woke up feeling nothing wrong. But after lunch, when I returned to the dorm, I suddenly felt the room was stiflingly hot. When I woke from a nap, my whole body was sore and cold. So this was what the virus we had feared for three years felt like after infection.

There was no medicine. Because control had been fully lifted, the school clinic no longer accepted fever cases. I decided to fight the virus with my body. I had no appetite, and when I woke again it was already 8 p.m. I stepped out of the dorm and found the hallway completely dark. Maybe everyone had already gone home; maybe they were all fighting the virus in their rooms.

In the end, I got on the subway home. As someone who had lived in Wenjiang for seven years, I had never seen Line 4 so empty on a Friday night. It felt strange to ride the subway for the first time without a health-code check, like a prisoner stepping out in *The Shawshank Redemption*: a little excited, and also somehow uneasy. The train rushed past one empty platform after another, and eventually I became the only passenger in that car.

Saturday was the hardest day. Severe headaches and muscle pain pinned me to the bed. Before sleeping that night, I took an ibuprofen and buried my head under the covers. Fortunately, I was young and strong. By match day, except for a faint hoarseness and a slight sting in my throat, the symptoms were gone. And even that little pain would have nowhere to hide once dopamine and adrenaline arrived together.

I reached the bar at 11 p.m. Before kickoff, I played games with friends and drank, but the alcohol did not do much. I wanted it to numb my nerves, but I was also afraid that my body, just recovered from the virus, might not withstand the burden. I knew that once the match started, I would shout for every attack and every defensive stop, explode for every goal, and feel my heart race after every goal conceded.

## December 18, Lusail

The night of December 18 at Lusail Stadium was destined for history.

The first thing that excited me was seeing the starting 4-3-3. Angel appeared on the left. Many pre-match predictions had expected Di Maria to start. He was born for finals and ultimate honors. I had imagined countless times that, like in 2014, he might bring the Pampas Eagles their third star with a beautiful chip.

But Scaloni placed him on the unfamiliar left, and for a while both media and professional commentators were confused.

The opening whistle of the final was like thunder tearing through Qatar's ink-dark sky. Every pass between the two teams felt like a probe touching the heart in my chest.

In the 23rd minute, Di Maria received the ball on the left, cut beautifully past Dembele, and entered the box. The future Ballon d'Or winner, the gaming king Ousmane whom I both love and hate, paid the price of youth. His right foot caught Di Maria's left foot after Angel had stepped into the penalty area. It was not a vicious foul, but how could an experienced veteran miss the chance to win a penalty? Angel fell, and the referee blew. An undisputed penalty.

You stood at the spot. The deep sockets under your brow bone and the shadows under the stadium lights made it hard for me to see whether your eyes were resolute. You ran up and shot. A change of rhythm made Lloris surrender his weight early. The ball rolled along the ground into the far-right corner.

In the 36th minute, a transition attack became Argentina's most beautiful counterattack of the tournament. With four passes, Angel again appeared in space on the left. Before Lloris could close him down, he lifted the ball lightly. It rose half a meter from the ground, passed over the goalkeeper's body by the smallest margin, and entered the net.

The miracle of the Angel descended again in a final. Di Maria ran and made his most familiar celebration. In that moment, to me and to fifty million Argentines, he truly was an angel. His thin cheeks and bronze muscle lines were far from the angels of Greek sculpture, but that beautiful goal felt like a special grace from God to this Catholic nation. And the person carrying that grace was the man with "Angel" in his name.

## When Fate Smiled Back

The first half ended 2-0. France, with all its talent, had not even created a threatening attack. Argentina's flowing offense had painted the field blue and white. Blue-and-white ghosts moved through the French midfield and back line. France looked like a drunken guest dancing tango with a gorgeous assassin, never knowing when a hidden blade under the skirt might cut.

But the defending champion was no ordinary opponent. France's deep talent pool gave Deschamps more moves to make. As the veterans' legs began to fade, France's youth storm rose around the 80th minute. Otamendi's pull on Kolo Muani gave Mbappe a penalty. It was like Kazan four years earlier. Even though Argentina still led 2-1 after the goal, intense unease rose inside me.

And sure enough, 97 seconds later, Mbappe received a lofted ball and struck it without letting it drop, sending it into the corner. On the field, a young man had scored twice and was shining. In the bar, French fans began to celebrate with abandon. The two teams had been dragged back to the same starting line. For the older Argentina side, victory suddenly felt farther away.

The camera cut to you after the turnover near the center circle. I do not know whether it was to ease embarrassment or because you had fallen again into that familiar uncertainty, but you lowered your head and adjusted your socks. Then you smiled once toward the camera.

I still do not know how to read that smile. It was like the smile you had given so many times before after missing chances, applauding your teammates. It also looked like a bitter smile, the kind that accepts the storm of fate and makes peace with tragedy.

But Argentines do not give up. These young players who once saw you as their idol were still running without fatigue. In the 108th minute, Lautaro's explosive shot rebounded to your feet. With one gentle push, the ball clearly crossed the line.

Watching live, I had not yet recovered from the feeling that my heart had skipped a beat. Then the camera cut to the referee: the green light on his watch, like a flame of hope; his hands pointing to the center circle; the whistle like heavenly music. That priceless goal pulled Argentina back from the edge of despair. If they could hold on for twelve more minutes, the World Cup trophy would be engraved with Argentina's name.

But heroes show themselves when the sea turns wild. Like Don Quixote charging forward, like Sisyphus pushing the boulder, fate seemed to tease you again. Montiel, defending with everything he had, blocked Mbappe's shot with his arm. Another undisputed penalty. When the young man scored it cleanly, the variation in fate's melody seemed to tell the world that perhaps tonight's protagonist still was not you.

You had tasted too much pain already: the 2014 World Cup final, three straight major-tournament runner-up finishes, the Anfield miracle against Liverpool in 2019, the 8-2 collapse against Bayern in 2020. And then, at the very end of extra time, an untidy clearance from the back gave France a golden chance. When Kolo Muani received the through ball and was nearly one-on-one, the whole world seemed to suffer cardiac arrest.

But Dibu stretched out his left leg and saved a shot that might have gone in ninety-nine times out of a hundred. At that moment, God seemed to look back toward the blue and white. Maybe it really was today.

After Dybala, coming off the bench, destroyed Mbappe's chance to shoot after he had slipped past Romero, the 120 minutes ended. Both sides had exhausted all physical strength and all tactics. The cruelest stage had arrived again: penalties.

## Twelve Yards

Rather than saying penalties are the final contest of strength, it is better to say they are a wager thrown by heaven like dice. One hundred and twenty minutes is enough to drain every athlete's body. At that point, the body is held up only by will.

Penalties are a man's game, like a duel in an arena. We have seen many legends wounded at the twelve-yard spot, and we have seen countless legends crowned there. Both teams had reasons they could not lose. But there could be only one champion.

In Chengdu, it was already 1:30 a.m. The street outside the bar was quiet, but thousands of lights were still on. I believed countless people were waiting in front of televisions for your coronation, while countless others were waiting for you to fail again.

The alcohol had disappeared. The virus had faded entirely. But my body was hotter and more excited than ever. During the break before penalties, I did not even dare go to the restroom, afraid I might miss something. I only remember that while scrolling through comments from extra time, my hands were shaking uncontrollably.

As always, you were the first to stand at the spot. Now the trophy was right in front of you. You had to consider whether this might be the only chance of your life. Pasternak said that a person lives only for a few moments, and perhaps this was one of the greatest moments of yours.

Again, you calmly shifted the goalkeeper's center of gravity and pushed the ball. Lloris guessed the direction at the last instant, but he was a fraction late and could not reach it. That touch felt like Zhang Sanfeng after mastering his art: a light move that looked effortless to the world, but was forged by sixteen years of training.

When Dibu saved Coman and Tchouameni's penalties in succession, the bar erupted. The golden cup felt almost within reach.

For the final kick, Montiel walked to the spot. Could this right back, who had given France the late penalty, complete his own redemption? If he missed and Argentina lost the trophy because of it, his name would be nailed forever to a pillar of shame. In South America, where football is life, he might be sent to hell for it.

But men are like this. No one who walks to the ball is a coward. Perhaps he had already imagined the consequences of missing. But he must also have understood the meaning of saving himself.

Run-up. Shot. The goalkeeper went the wrong way.

Another extremely simple, plain penalty. The Argentine defender from River Plate, a man who had never missed a penalty, took the final kick of a World Cup final shootout and brought a third championship to a nation that had waited thirty-six years.

Argentines were waiting for a champion. I was waiting for the king of football to be formally crowned.

I can no longer remember exactly how I celebrated after the match. It feels as if all language fails there. Even this thousand-word essay cannot reproduce one percent of that match's brilliance and beauty.

## What Stayed With Me

I am writing these words nearly four years after that championship, but I can still recall so many details. I remember your smile as you kissed the World Cup trophy. The golden cup looked like your lovely fourth child, obediently waiting to be touched by the king of football. The image of you walking toward your teammates while holding the trophy carried an indescribable beauty.

The cup in your hands looked like the football at your feet. You were born for this sport, and you were born for championships.

The happiness of that night is carved permanently into memory. It is hard for any other memory to surpass that mixture of feelings. It was an explosion after long suppression. It was a sincere, beautiful, firm affirmation of what had happened. It felt like the perfect full stop to youth, and also like a thrilling preface to a new chapter of life.

The fireworks over Qatar lit up the winter of 2022. They warmed me and countless people who loved you. Wave after wave of cheers comforted a young college freshman's confusion and pushed back against the absurdity and alienation brought by the pandemic disaster.

Football gave meaning to Argentina, that tragic nation. And this legendary journey became medicine for me in that difficult winter. Some parts of that beautiful fairy tale have been engraved deep inside me. They have become part of my character, and they will keep shaping me on the road ahead.
